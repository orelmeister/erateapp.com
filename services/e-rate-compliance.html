Six Critical E-Rate Compliance Areas We Monitor

Each area carries its own rules, deadlines, and risks. We track all of them so you don't have to.

1

CIPA Compliance

The Children's Internet Protection Act (CIPA) is a non-negotiable requirement for E-Rate eligibility. Without it, your entire application is invalid.

  • Internet content filtering on all school/library-owned devices
  • Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) adoption and enforcement
  • Internet safety education for students
  • Technology protection measures documentation
2

Document Retention

The FCC requires E-Rate participants to retain all program-related records for at least 5 years after the last date of service. Missing documents during an audit can result in funding recovery.

  • Form 470, 471, and all supporting documentation
  • Competitive bidding records (RFPs, bid evaluations, vendor communications)
  • Contracts, invoices, and proof of payment
  • USAC correspondence and PIA review responses
  • Technology plans and board meeting minutes
3

Competitive Bidding Compliance

E-Rate's competitive bidding rules are among the strictest in federal procurement. A procedural misstep can invalidate your entire application.

  • 28-day minimum posting period for Form 470
  • Fair and open evaluation of all bids received
  • Price of eligible services as the primary factor
  • Proper documentation of vendor selection rationale
  • No pre-selection or bid manipulation
4

Post-Funding Compliance

Winning E-Rate funding is only half the battle. After your FCDL is issued, strict post-funding requirements must be met to actually receive your money.

  • BEAR (Billed Entity Applicant Reimbursement) form filing
  • SPI (Service Provider Invoice) coordination with vendors
  • Asset inventory and tracking for Category 2 equipment
  • Invoice deadline compliance (October 28 of following year)
  • Service delivery verification
5

Gift Rules & Vendor Relationships

FCC rules strictly govern the relationship between E-Rate applicants and service providers. Even well-intentioned interactions can trigger compliance violations.

  • No gifts above $20 nominal value from vendors
  • No vendor-provided meals, trips, or entertainment
  • Arms-length vendor negotiations required
  • Prohibition on vendor involvement in the application process
  • Conflict of interest disclosure requirements
6

Audit Readiness

USAC conducts audits, selective reviews, and beneficiary & contributor audits (BACA) that can examine years of past applications. Being unprepared can cost you current and past funding.

  • USAC Payment Quality Assurance (PQA) audits
  • Selective reviews during application processing
  • FCC Enforcement Bureau investigations
  • Inspector General inquiries
  • Proactive internal compliance assessments
