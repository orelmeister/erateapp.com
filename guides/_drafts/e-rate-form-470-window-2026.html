1. What is Form 470?

Form 470 (officially: "Description of Services Requested and Certification Form") is the document that opens the E-Rate competitive bidding process. Filing Form 470 tells the marketplace — and USAC — that your school, school district, library, library system, or consortium intends to purchase eligible products or services with E-Rate discounts, and that you are inviting service providers to submit bids.

You must file Form 470 if:

  • You are requesting E-Rate discounts on any service that is not already covered by an active multi-year contract that was bid through a prior valid Form 470.
  • You are starting a new service, switching providers, or expanding scope.
  • Your current contract has expired or is about to expire and you intend to apply for discounts in the upcoming funding year.

You may not need a new Form 470 if:

  • You are continuing an existing multi-year contract that is still active and still has voluntary extension years that were disclosed in the original Form 470.
  • You are requesting tariffed or month-to-month services for less than $3,600 per year (the FCC's de minimis threshold) and your state procurement rules do not require competitive bidding.

The applicant who certifies Form 470 must be an authorized representative of the billed entity — typically a superintendent, business manager, technology director, library director, or designated E-Rate coordinator. The certification is legally binding and is one of the documents USAC will request during a Program Integrity Assurance (PIA) review.

3. Step-by-Step Filing Instructions

You file Form 470 inside the EPC (E-Rate Productivity Center) portal at portal.usac.org/em. Here is the sequence we use with every consulting client.

Step 1 — Confirm your Billed Entity Number (BEN) and EPC profile

Log into EPC and verify your billed entity number, NSLP percentages, urban/rural designation, and student count. These numbers determine your discount percentage and are auto-pulled into Form 471 later. Outdated profile data is the single biggest cause of mid-application delays.

Step 2 — Confirm your authorized contact information

The Form 470 contact person is the one who will receive bid inquiries from vendors. Use a real, monitored email — not a shared mailbox that no one checks. Vendor questions must be answered consistently across all bidders to satisfy fair-and-open bidding rules.

Step 3 — Choose your service category

Select Category 1 (broadband, internet access, voice/data transport) or Category 2 (internal connections, managed Wi-Fi, basic maintenance of internal connections, network upgrades), or both. You can request multiple service types on a single Form 470, but each must have a clear, narrative description.

Step 4 — Write clear service descriptions

For each service, describe what you need in plain, vendor-neutral language. Include bandwidth, term, locations, technical requirements, and any state procurement constraints. Avoid brand names unless absolutely necessary — naming a brand without "or equivalent" can be flagged as restrictive bidding.

Step 5 — Attach your RFP (if applicable)

If your project is complex or your state procurement rules require an RFP, upload the document directly to EPC or provide a public URL where bidders can download it. The RFP must be available to all bidders for the full 28-day window.

Step 6 — Certify and submit

The authorized signer reviews the certification text, confirms the descriptions are accurate, and submits. Once submitted, the form is public and the 28-day clock starts on the date the form is posted.

Step 7 — Maintain a bid evaluation matrix

From the day you post, set up your scoring matrix. Price must be the most heavily weighted single factor (per FCC rules), but you may include other weighted factors such as past performance, technical capability, support response time, and references. Document every vendor communication in writing.

4. Common Mistakes That Get Form 470 Denied

Most Form 470 denials are caused by procedural errors that are entirely preventable. Read our 10 Common E-Rate Mistakes guide for the full breakdown, but the Form 470-specific landmines to avoid are:

  • Signing a contract before the 28-day window closes. The Allowable Contract Date is non-negotiable. Even one day early voids the entire competitive bidding process.
  • Naming a specific brand without "or equivalent." This is treated as restrictive bidding unless your state procurement law allows brand-specific sourcing.
  • Communicating differently with different vendors. Anything you tell one bidder must be shared with all bidders, in writing, with a date stamp.
  • Missing or contradictory service descriptions. If your Form 470 says "managed Wi-Fi" but your contract says "switches and routers," PIA will flag it.
  • Filing Form 471 before day 29. Even if bids came in on day 5, you cannot certify Form 471 until at least 28 days after the Form 470 posting date.
  • Not posting your RFP at the same time as Form 470. Late RFPs reset the 28-day clock.

5. 28-Day Bid Window Compliance

The 28-day window is the heart of the E-Rate competitive bidding requirement. Treat it as a regulated procurement event, not a formality.

Fair and open communication

From the moment you post Form 470 until the day you announce your selected vendor, you must accept inquiries from every interested service provider on equal terms. Best practice: route all bid-related communication through a single email address, archive every message, and respond to the entire bidder list — not just the asker — whenever you clarify a specification.

No sole-source selections

Unless your annual request is below the FCC's de minimis threshold (currently $3,600), sole-source contracts are not permitted. If only one vendor bids, document that you advertised broadly, accepted bids from any interested party, and received only one response. USAC will accept a single-bid result if the process was fair.

Evaluation criteria must be set before bids arrive

Write down your evaluation matrix before the bid window closes — ideally before you post Form 470. Price must be the single most heavily weighted factor. Other factors might include technical merit, past performance, references, customer support, and ability to meet timelines. Score each bid against the same matrix and keep the scored matrix in your records for at least ten years.

Document, document, document

USAC's PIA reviewers expect: the Form 470 itself, the RFP (if any), every vendor communication, your bid evaluation matrix with scores, a winning-vendor selection memo, the contract signed on or after day 29, and a board-approval record (if your state requires one). Missing any of these is a top denial trigger.

6. After Form 470 — What Comes Next

Form 470 is just step one of the E-Rate application sequence. Here is what happens after the 28-day window closes.

  1. Score the bids. Apply your matrix and select the winning vendor. If price is tied or close, document why the higher-cost bid wins (technical merit, reference quality, etc.).
  2. Write a vendor selection memo. A short internal document that explains who won, why, and how their bid compared to the others. This is gold during a PIA review.
  3. Sign the contract on or after day 29. Note: month-to-month tariffed services do not require a signed contract.
  4. File Form 471 during the funding year application window. See our Form 471 deadline guide for the exact sequence and our Form 470 deep dive for the full procedural playbook.
  5. Prepare for PIA. USAC will review your Form 471 and may ask follow-up questions. Pull your Form 470, RFP, bid evaluation matrix, and contract into a single folder before PIA opens.
  6. Receive the FCDL. When PIA finishes, USAC issues a Funding Commitment Decision Letter (FCDL). If approved, you can start using services. If denied, you have 60 days to appeal.

7. Frequently Asked Questions

When does the FY2027 Form 470 window open?

July 1, 2026. From that date forward, applicants may post a Form 470 in EPC to begin the 28-day competitive bidding process.

How long does Form 470 have to be posted before I can sign a contract?

At least 28 calendar days. Day 29 (the Allowable Contract Date) is the earliest you can evaluate bids, select a winner, and sign.

Can I file Form 470 before July 1?

No. EPC will not accept FY2027 Form 470 submissions until July 1, 2026.

What happens if I file Form 470 late?

You may not be able to complete the 28-day window in time to certify Form 471 before the spring 2027 deadline, which means losing funding for FY2027 unless you qualify for a USAC appeal or waiver.

Do I need a Form 470 every year?

Only if your existing multi-year contract has expired, was not bid through a valid prior Form 470, or no longer covers the scope you need. Otherwise, continuing requests can ride the original Form 470.

Can I list both Category 1 and Category 2 services on one Form 470?

Yes, but USAC suggests separating them when the vendor pools or project types are very different.

What is a Form 470 RFP and is it required?

An RFP is a detailed specification document. It is required when your project is complex or when your state procurement rules require one. The RFP must be available to all bidders for the entire 28-day window.

Who can I contact about my Form 470 during the bid window?

Any interested service provider can contact you. All communications must be documented, and any clarification given to one bidder must be shared with all bidders.

Can I cancel a Form 470 after I post it?

Yes, you can withdraw it in EPC, but re-posting restarts the 28-day clock.

What is the difference between Form 470 and Form 471?

Form 470 announces your need and opens the bid window. Form 471 is the funding request that names the winning vendor and the discount you are requesting, filed after the 28-day window closes and after the contract is signed.
